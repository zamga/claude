# Plimsoll: competitive landscape and blue ocean strategy

*Research date: 1 October 2026. Prices and features come from vendor pages, review sites and search results gathered that day; items marked ⚠ could not be confirmed against a primary source.*

## 1. The red ocean

Company analysis and valuation tools fight on the same factors: more data, more screens, more scores.

| Group | Who | Price (USD) | How value is shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional terminals | Bloomberg, S&P Capital IQ Pro, FactSet, LSEG Workspace | ≈ $12k–32k per seat a year | Data, consensus and comparables; the analyst builds the DCF in Excel |
| Retail terminals | Koyfin, TIKR, Stock Rover, GuruFocus | Free to ≈ $2,400 a year | Multiples, consensus-prefilled models; GuruFocus has a numeric reverse-DCF panel |
| Visual valuation apps | Simply Wall St, Alpha Spread, ValueInvesting.io, Fiscal.ai | Free to ≈ $260 a year | One fair value and an “x% undervalued” bar; Simply Wall St adds the Snowflake and community Narratives |
| Ratings services | Morningstar Investor, Seeking Alpha Premium, Stockopedia | ≈ $250–400 a year | Stars, quant grades and StockRanks; Morningstar’s uncertainty rating is the best mainstream treatment of ranges |
| AI assistants | Perplexity Finance, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini | ≈ $20 a month | Fluent prose and one-off models that do not persist; unreliable figures without live data |
| DIY | Excel templates, Damodaran’s and Mauboussin’s free spreadsheets | Free | Fully transparent, but slow, error-prone and intimidating |

**Category clichés** (what nearly everyone does): a dark multi-panel “terminal”; red/green tickers; compressed scores (stars, grades, 0–100, snowflakes); one headline fair value with an “undervalued” bar; screeners with hundreds of filters; an AI chat panel added to everything; auto-generated “Is X undervalued?” articles.

**Recurring complaints**: cost and learning curve (terminals); opaque or “clearly off” automatic fair values and conflicting numbers across the same platform (visual apps); verdicts that cannot be questioned (ratings); wrong figures (AI assistants); manual entry and errors (spreadsheets).

## 2. Unmet jobs (the open water)

1. **“What does today’s price assume, in plain words?”** Reverse DCF exists only as a number (GuruFocus) or a manual goal-seek tutorial (TIKR). Nobody draws it, and nobody compares it with what the company has actually delivered.
2. **“How sure can I be?”** No mainstream retail tool runs a Monte Carlo. Ranges appear only as bull/base/bear cases or categorical bands.
3. **“Turn my story into numbers, with guardrails.”** Simply Wall St Narratives come closest; none links a story to reinvestment needs and a moat.
4. **“Value a company that isn’t listed.”** Founders, startup employees and small-business owners are left with a paid 409A or a spreadsheet.
5. **“Share a valuation someone can open and change.”** Outputs are screenshots, PDFs and paywalled pages.

## 3. Non-customers

| Tier | Who | Why they stay away | What brings them in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soon-to-be | Investors deciding through social feeds and chatbots; 35% of investors under 30 rely on social media (FINRA Foundation 2025) | Distrust black-box verdicts; no time for terminals | One sentence on what the price assumes; a chart they can share |
| Refusing | People intimidated by statements and spreadsheets; professionals priced out of terminal seats | Jargon, learning curve, cost | A picture that teaches valuation by doing; free core |
| Unexplored | Employees holding equity (7–10M US employees receive options, NCEO ⚠); founders and owners (41% of RBC business-owner clients have never had a valuation); students; journalists | No tool accepts a private company; 409A reports cost ≈ $1–5k | “Survey your own”: seven figures, a full chart, a shareable link |

Plimsoll’s value curve runs low where the industry competes (breadth, speed, screens, verdicts) and high where no group scores 7 or more at the same time (transparency, ranges, implied expectations and ease).

## 5. Four actions (ERRC)

| Eliminate | Reduce |
|---|---|
| Ticker tape, price alerts, news feeds | Data breadth: the dozen numbers that move value, not five hundred |
| Buy/sell ratings, stars, grades, snowflakes | Screening: an atlas you browse, not a filter wall |
| A single fair value quoted to the cent | Jargon: every nautical term carries its plain finance name |
| Stock-photo bulls and bears | Chrome: one chart, one gauge, one reading |

| Raise | Create |
|---|---|
| Assumption transparency: every input on show and editable | **The coastline**: market-implied expectations drawn across growth × margin |
| Honesty about uncertainty: odds, not verdicts | **Odds on dry land**: the share of your own range of beliefs worth more than the price |
| Methodological rigour: Damodaran-style FCFF with reinvestment and a moat | **Narratives as presets**: six stories that set growth, margin, timing and moat |
| Craft: a tool people want to open | **Survey your own**: any private company from seven figures |
| Privacy: theses stay in the browser | **Shareable charts**: a link that reproduces a chart exactly, and a printable chart image |

## 6. Positioning

> For anyone who wants to know what a company is worth and what its price assumes, Plimsoll is the valuation instrument that draws value as terrain and the price as sea level, so the market’s story and yours sit on one chart. Unlike terminals and rating apps, it gives no verdicts: it shows the coastline, your bearing and the odds.

**Tagline:** The price is sea level. **Sign-off:** Know the line.

## 7. Business model (assumption, for validation)

| Tier | Price | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | The atlas, any SEC filer surveyed live, survey your own, sharing |
| Pro | ≈ $9 a month | Saved charts as a thesis log, drift alerts when a new filing moves the survey, chart images without the footer, CSV export |
| Classroom | per seat | Assignments built on charts; the student tier of non-customers |
| API and MCP | usage | Charts and implied expectations inside AI assistants, the channel the research flags as both threat and route to market |

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| “Everything is under water” when rates are high | Visitors read the tool as bearish | Defaults are explained beside every input; the reading leads with the market’s story, not a verdict |
| Data errors in an automated survey | Trust | Sources and notes on every chart; refresh is reviewed like code |
| Nautical vocabulary confuses | Usability | Every term carries its plain name (“Freeboard · margin of safety”); a glossary on the Method page |
| Regulatory perception of advice | Legal | No ratings or recommendations; a model, clearly labelled, not advice |
