§ 1

Thesis

Every mispricing has a reason. A founder with no successor. A lender out of patience. An asset nobody has valued in twenty years. We find the reason, price it, and wait write the terms that close the gap.

ICInvestment Committee

If we can’t answer 1.2, we don’t have an edge. We have an opinion.

  1. 1.1

    What is mispriced?

    The asset, cash flow or right the market is valuing wrong, and by how much.

  2. 1.2

    Why does the gap persist?

    Complexity, time pressure, stigma or scale. If we can’t name the friction, there is no edge.

  3. 1.3

    What closes it?

    A catalyst with a date on it: a refinancing, a retirement, a consolidation, a contract renewal.

  4. 1.4

    Why us?

    Speed, certainty and structure. We can say yes where other buyers can only say “subject to”.

§ 2

Where the edge lives

Six frictions keep good businesses mispriced. Each one has a structure that resolves it.

  1. (a)

    Succession

    Founder-led businesses with no heir and no plan. We buy time as much as equity.

    Vendor note · Rollover

  2. (b)

    Time pressure

    Deadlines compress price. We underwrite in weeks and close without a financing condition.

    Certain funds

  3. (c)

    Fragmentation

    Eleven operators trade at 4–5×. One platform trades at 9×. The spread pays for the work.

    Buy-and-build

  4. (d)

    Capital structure

    Good businesses under the wrong balance sheet. We buy the debt and own the outcome.

    Credit-to-own · Recap

  5. (e)

    Idle assets

    Property, IP and licences carried at cost and ignored at value.

    Sale-leaseback · Carve-out

  6. (f)

    Contracts

    Options, consents and change-of-control clauses that nobody has read.

    Options · Earn-outs

§ 3

Asymmetry, engineered.

Five steps, always in this order. Any one of them can kill a deal, and that is the point.

Plate 3.1The situation, taken apart
Illustrative1 / 5
  1. 3.1

    Deconstruct

    Assets, cash flows, liabilities, rights, control, time and incentives. We take the situation apart to first principles before anyone puts a number on it.

    OutputA map of who owns, controls, funds and gets paid, and when.

  2. 3.2

    Forensics

    Reported EBITDA is a claim. We rebuild it line by line: owner pay, one-offs, capitalised costs, related parties, receivables that will never be collected. Every finding moves the price or the protection.

    OutputNormalised EBITDA, and cash flow after tax, capex and working capital.

  3. 3.3

    Structure

    We separate ownership, control, financing and payment across time. Vendor notes, earn-outs, options and rollover give both sides a reason to say yes.

    OutputSources and uses, and a reason for every party to sign.

  4. 3.4

    Underwrite

    Downside, base and upside. We name the weakest assumption and fix the walk-away price before the first term sheet goes out.

    OutputThe highest price we can justify, and the price we won’t pay.

  5. 3.5

    Execute

    Certain funds, minimum liquidity, covenants and a dated path to close. A thesis is only worth what the closing is worth.

    OutputA signed SPA, funded, on the date we said.
